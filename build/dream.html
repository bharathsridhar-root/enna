Dream machines

Enna is a hand squeezed pump pushing oil through a hole. That is one point in a very large space. This page is the rest of the space, borrowed from paint shops, crop sprayers, jet engines, snack factories and inhalers.

Read this as a wish list, not a plan. The product pages carry commitments. This one carries none. Several of these would be better products and worse businesses, and a few are simply beautiful and wrong.
01

There are only six ways to break a liquid apart

Every atomiser ever built uses one of these, or two in combination. Once you see the list, the category stops looking like a crowded shelf and starts looking like one occupied corner of an empty room.

Borrowed from: perfume, garden sprayers

1. Pressure

Force liquid through a small hole fast enough that it tears itself into droplets. Simple, cheap, no power. The hole has to be small, which is exactly why every sprayer in this category dies of clogging.

This is what Enna does today.

Borrowed from: jet engines, oil burners

2. Gas assist

Mix air into the liquid before the orifice. The bubbles expand on the way out and shatter the oil from the inside. Because the gas does the work, the passages can be large and the pressure low.

The best fit nobody in this category uses.

Borrowed from: car paint booths, spray drying

3. Centrifugal

Drip oil onto a disc spinning at thousands of rpm and let it fling itself off the edge into droplets. There is no orifice at all. Used industrially precisely because pastes and slurries plug nozzles.

Structurally cannot clog.

Borrowed from: crop dusting, snack seasoning

4. Electrical

Charge the droplets and ground the target. The mist is pulled onto the pan and wraps around it instead of drifting into the room. Real food industry technology, not a lab curiosity.

Cuts the oil again, needs a grounded pan.

Borrowed from: humidifiers, asthma inhalers

5. Acoustic

Vibrate a mesh at ultrasonic frequency and shake droplets off it. It is the obvious modern answer and it is the one that clearly does not work here: common piezo atomisers cannot aerosolise viscous fluids, and struggle above about 6 cP. Cooking oil is 40 to 90.

Ruled out on physics, not on cost.

Borrowed from: nothing. This is cheating.

6. Change the liquid

Do not build a better atomiser. Make the oil easier to atomise. Warm it by twenty degrees and the viscosity roughly halves, and the fats we publicly cannot spray become fats we can.

The cheapest physics on this page.

03

Four machines worth dreaming about

Each of these is a real product, not a concept render. Each also has a reason it might never ship, written directly underneath.

Dream one · closest to buildable

The effervescent trigger

Identical to Enna from the outside. Same squeeze, same steel, same modules. Inside, the trigger drives a small air piston as well as the oil piston, and the air is injected into the oil upstream of the orifice. The bubbles expand on the way out and blow the oil apart from within.

This is well established twin fluid atomisation, used precisely because it processes highly viscous liquids into a fine spray with low gas consumption. Published work reaches roughly 25 µm droplets at viscosities up to 400 mPa·s, at gas pressures of 1.4 to 2.8 bar. Cooking oil is 37 to 83 mPa·s and our pre-compression valve already cracks at 2.4 bar. The numbers line up almost uncomfortably well.

What changesA second piston and a mixing chamber
What it buysPassages ten times larger
PowerStill none. Your hand
Where it is usedOil burners, spray drying
Why it might not ship

Effervescent sprays are known to be unsteady, and the character of the spray depends on the gas to liquid ratio. A hand squeeze does not hold a constant ratio, so the first pull and the last pull of a stroke may not look the same. That is an engineering problem, not a physics problem, which is exactly the kind worth attempting.

Dream two · the one that ends clogging forever

The hand cranked spinning disc

Squeeze the trigger and a gear train spins a small disc to ten thousand rpm. Oil drips onto the centre of the disc, spreads into a film, and flings itself off the rim as droplets. There is no orifice anywhere in the machine. Nothing to block, nothing to descale, nothing to poke a pin through.

Centrifugal atomisation is used industrially for exactly this reason: it handles suspensions and pastes that erode or plug nozzles. And there is a lovely inversion at the heart of it. In nozzles, thicker oil sprays worse. On a spinning disc, higher viscosity promotes a more stable spreading film and can produce finer droplets. The physics runs the right way for once.

What changesPump becomes a gear train
What it buysClogging stops existing
PowerNone, if hand cranked
Same gestureYou still just squeeze
Why it might not ship

A gear train to 10,000 rpm from a hand squeeze is a real mechanism with real friction, real noise and real wear, and it is the opposite of six simple modules. It also throws a wide, soft cloud rather than a directed cone, so it wants a shroud, and a shroud is a thing that gets oily and needs cleaning. You would trade the clog for a cowling.

Dream three · the one that breaks our own promise

The warm sprayer

A 12 watt heating element in the base cap brings the oil to about 45 °C before it reaches the pump. Viscosity roughly halves. Every atomiser on this page works better, the existing nozzle works better, and the two fats the product page honestly admits it cannot handle become fats it can.

Coconut oil sets below 24 °C and ghee is solid for much of the Indian year. That limitation is currently printed on our own website as a virtue of honesty. Thirty seconds of gentle warming makes it untrue, which would be the single largest expansion of what this product is for.

What changesA PTC element and a USB-C port
What it buysGhee, coconut, winter
Power10 to 15 W, rechargeable
BonusHelps every other dream here
Why it might not ship

It puts a battery, a heater and a charging port into a product whose entire case is that it is simple, modular and repairable for ten years. Warm oil also oxidises faster than cold oil, so a feature bought to serve one honesty claim quietly damages another. And it becomes an appliance, which is a different shelf, a different price and a different set of safety approvals.

Dream four · not a sprayer at all

The oil stick

A push up tube, exactly like a deodorant or a glue stick, holding solidified coconut oil or ghee. You wind out two millimetres and wipe it round a warm pan. It melts on contact and spreads further than any spray, because the pan does the work.

It has no pump, no nozzle, no seals, no screens and no moving parts beyond a screw thread. It cannot clog, cannot leak, and doses more precisely than any atomiser, because a marked thread is a far better metering device than a human hand. It solves the exact problem the sprayer cannot, using the property of solid fats that makes them a problem in the first place.

What changesEverything. It is a new product
What it buysGhee, butter, coconut
PowerNone. Barely a mechanism
ModulesThree
Why it might not ship

It is a second product, not a variant, and it competes for the same tooling budget. It also asks people to buy fat in a format they have never bought it in, which is a far harder ask than a better bottle. The honest read is that this is a second company, or a second year.

04

Five more, unranked and unjustified

Kept here because the point of this page is to be a net, not a filter.

  • Electrostatic assist. Charge the mist, ground the pan through the hob. Food industry trials moved transfer yield from about 17% to about 40%, and electrostatic seasoning systems claim up to 45% less powder. If it held here it would cut oil use again on top of the spray, which is the only idea on this page that attacks the calorie number rather than the clog. Needs kilovolts near a hot surface and a grounded pan, so treat it as a laboratory question first.
  • Peristaltic dosing. A motor squeezes a silicone tube and the oil touches nothing else in the machine. Cleaning becomes replacing a ten rupee tube, and dosing becomes exact rather than approximate. It gives up the mist entirely and becomes a precision drizzler, which may be a better product for baking than for a pan.
  • Collapsible liner. A bag inside the bottle that shrinks as oil leaves, so no air ever enters. Oxidation stops being a design problem and becomes an impossibility. It fights the refillable, repairable premise, because the bag is a consumable.
  • Oil sheets. Thin dissolvable squares of solidified oil, one per pan, like a dishwasher tablet. Perfect portion control, zero mechanism, wonderful packaging story, and an enormous behaviour change to ask for.
  • Micro heater bubble nucleation. The one route that makes ultrasonics work on viscous fluids: tiny heaters nucleate bubbles which do the atomising instead of the vibration. Published for aromatherapy oils. Beautiful, and at least a decade from a kitchen.
05

What I would actually do with this page

Build the effervescent trigger as a bench rig, not a product. It is the only dream here that keeps every commitment the real product has made. Same gesture, same six modules, no power, no battery, no appliance. It needs a second piston and a mixing chamber, and the published numbers for viscosity and pressure already sit where we need them. A rig on a bench would answer it in a month for the price of a prototype round.

Treat the spinning disc as the patent, not the product. A hand cranked rotary atomiser for kitchen oil is genuinely novel and genuinely defensible. Filing it costs a fraction of building it, and it protects a direction we may want in five years and certainly do not want a competitor to own.

Keep the warm sprayer for the professional line. A commercial kitchen already has power everywhere, does not care about a charging port, and has the one use case that justifies the whole thing, which is ghee. The objection that it becomes an appliance stops being an objection in a kitchen full of appliances.

Leave the oil stick alone until Enna ships. It is the most charming idea on this page and it is a different company. Write it down, date it, and come back.
