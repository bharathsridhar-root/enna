The gauge

It counts, so you don't have to.

No window, no glass, no hole in the wall to leak from. A mechanical counter in the head ticks once per stroke — the same movement an inhaler uses. Because every stroke is exactly 0.20 ml, it is at once a fuel gauge and a calorie meter.

Where the name comes from

"என்ன வேணும்?"

In Tamil, enna means what. Move one sound and எண்ணெய் — ennai — means oil. There is a comedy scene every Tamil household can recite, built entirely on those two words refusing to meet. We named the product after the collision.

Customer எண்ணெய் வேணும்! eṇṇai vēṇum! Oil is what I want!

Why a sprayer at all

The same pan. Four ways to oil it.

This is the entire argument for the product, and it is just arithmetic. Cooking oil carries about 9 kcal per gram and weighs about 0.92 g per millilitre — so calories are simply a question of how many millilitres end up in the pan.

One Enna stroke meters 0.20 ml — 0.18 g — about 1.7 kcal. Coating a 26 cm pan takes eight to ten strokes. The claim isn't that a spray is magic; it's that a spray is measurable, and a pour never is.

The India problem

Three stages against the clog.

Indian kitchens strain and reuse frying oil, and what comes back carries besan, crumbs and masala. That particulate is what kills every sprayer on the market inside a month. It has three separate causes, so Enna answers it in three separate places — in the order the oil meets them.

What we fixed

Nine complaints, nine answers.

Each of these is a documented failure in reviews of the sprayers already on sale — the ₹300 imports and the ₹2,600 imports alike. We treated the review sections as a specification.

01

It clogs in two weeks

Three-stage filtration with a raised intake, so the particulate never reaches the pump in the first place.

02

It sprays a jet, not a mist

A pre-compression valve holds a 2.4 bar floor under every discharge. Squeeze slowly and it still atomises — or it doesn't fire at all.

03

It leaks at the thread

Coarse four-start threads that cannot cross-thread, closing onto silicone face seals rather than thread-on-thread contact.

04

You can't clean inside it

Both ends open. The body becomes a straight tube you can see through, brush through and drop in hot water.

05

Oil runs down the outside

The nozzle is recessed in a wiper collar with a return channel that drains the post-spray bead back into the body.

06

The plastic top warped

Steel and silicone in every part that faces the hob. Nothing near the heat is structural plastic.

07

It's a mess to refill

A one-litre pouch empties into a Ø58 mm mouth without a funnel, and 500 ml means the pouch goes in two fills, not six.

08

You never know what's left

A mechanical stroke counter in the head, not a window. No hole in the wall means no leak path — and it counts calories while it's there.

09

Nobody answers the warranty

Spares sold openly at ₹199 — nozzle, both gaskets, both screens — so "buy once" means something past year two.

Honestly

What Enna will not do.

Every sprayer sold in India today claims to handle every oil. None of them do. We would rather draw the line ourselves than let you discover it in January.

Solid coconut oil

Coconut oil sets below about 24 °C. No sprayer on earth atomises a solid. Warm the bottle in a bowl of hot water, or use it in the months it stays liquid.

Ghee

Solid at Indian room temperature for much of the year. Enna is built for liquid oils — mustard, groundnut, gingelly, sunflower, rice bran. Ghee belongs on a spoon.

Unstrained frying oil

The filtration is designed for what survives a normal kitchen strainer, not for oil poured straight back out of the kadai with the crumbs still in it.

Make old oil fresh

An opaque steel body slows ageing — it doesn't stop it. 500 ml is about eight weeks of normal cooking, which is roughly the right window. Keep the rest of the pouch sealed and out of the light.
