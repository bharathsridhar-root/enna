01

Architecture

Six modules. Not eleven parts. The difference is that a module is a thing you can buy, stock, diagnose and swap without knowing anything about what is inside it.

The earlier specification had eleven individually replaceable parts, which is maximally repairable and practically useless: eleven SKUs to stock, eleven things to lose in a sink, and a customer who has to work out which one failed. Six modules is the point where repairability stops being a slogan.

Nothing is glued, welded, riveted or captive. Threads and snap fits only. Every module comes apart by hand and goes back the same way.

What the fixed nozzle changed

No mode dial, and the product got better

A rotating multi position collar puts a moving seal in the oil path, which is both a wear part and a leak path, and it needs internal channels that make the head harder to mould and impossible to service. Removing it cost us two features and the architecture replaced both.

Clearing a clog was going to be a PURGE detent. It is now pull the head, flush it, click it back. Five seconds, no tools, no bypass channel. Pouring a spoonful was going to be a DRIZZLE detent. It is now what the decanter is for. The second vessel answers the 2 in 1 objection that a single bottle needed a dial to answer.

02

The repairability commitment

This is a promise with a ten year tail, so it is written down here in the form it will be published, and it constrains the design rather than describing it.

What we commit to

A public exploded drawing and a part number for every module. Not a marketing render. A dimensioned drawing with the module boundaries, the fits, and the order code.

Parts stocked for ten years from last shipment. Every module, plus gaskets, remains orderable. If we ever cannot supply one, we publish the drawing in enough detail for somebody else to.

No adhesives, no ultrasonic welds, no rivets, nothing captive. Threads and snap fits only. This is the constraint that does real work: it rules out the cheapest assembly method at almost every joint, and it is why the head is a housing around a pump core rather than a pump potted into a shell.

A repair QR on the body that resolves to the drawing set and the reorder page, so the documentation is attached to the object rather than to a manual nobody keeps.

Why publish rather than just sell spares

Because a promise nobody can check is marketing, and every ₹300 import already claims to be durable. A published drawing set is verifiable, and it is the one claim in this category that a competitor cannot make cheaply, because making it requires actually designing the product this way.

It is not open source hardware. We publish what a repairer needs, not the full CAD, tolerances and process notes an import factory would need to clone the geometry. The distinction matters and we should hold it.

03

The interface specification

The head to body interface is published. Anything built to it will fit, including things we do not make.

Modularity that only lets you buy our parts is not modularity, it is a spares business with better vocabulary. Publishing the interface is what makes the claim real, and it costs us very little, because the value in this product is the pump core and the filtration, not the thread form.

What gets published

The body mouth thread, four start, Ø48 mm, with its pitch, lead and tolerance band. The face seal land and its required flatness. The nozzle bayonet form. The intake cartridge register and its clearance. Enough for somebody to make a vessel our head screws onto, or a nozzle that fits our head.

What it buys us

Future sizes cost one body tool. A 500 ml body for people who cook more, or a 1 litre body for a professional kitchen, reuses MOD-01, 02, 03, 05 and 06 unchanged. The head becomes the platform and the bottle becomes the variable, which inverts how every competitor in this category is structured.

The food service version stops being a different product. The earlier assessment said a professional unit needs 500 ml to a litre and no counter, and called that a different product. With a published interface it is a different body.

Accessories become other people's problem. If somebody makes a nozzle for vinegar, or a wall dock, or a glass body for people who want to see the level, that is reach we did not pay for.

The risk, stated plainly

A published thread makes it easier for an import to make a compatible body and undercut MOD-04. That is a real cost and we should accept it, because the body is ₹92 of BOM and the head is ₹228, and a customer who buys a cheap third party body still needs our head, our nozzle and our intake cartridge. Publishing the interface concedes the cheap half to defend the expensive half.

Versioning: the interface gets a version number and a commitment not to break it. If it ever has to change, both versions ship in parallel for two years.

05

The anti-clog system

Clogging kills every sprayer on the market, and it has three separate causes, so it gets three separate answers in the order the oil meets them.

Stage 1, passive. Raised intake, 9 mm standoff

The pickup never touches the floor. Sediment settles into the moat beneath it and is never drawn up. This is fuel-tank logic and it costs nothing: it is a dimension, not a component. It is also the stage that does the most work, because particulate that never enters the tube cannot clog anything downstream.

Stage 2, serviceable. 600 µm bayonet intake basket

A quarter-turn stainless basket the user pulls out and rinses. The Japanese market already ships filters in the dip tube, which validates the idea, but theirs are sealed, so when they clog they stay clogged. Ours is the same idea made maintainable.

Stage 3, last defence. 150 µm fine screen

Sits directly behind the orifice, where a single grain ruins the spray cone. Anything that survives stage 2 stops here.

Why 600 and 150 µm

These are design targets, not validated numbers. The intake is sized to stop what survives a normal domestic strainer, besan lumps, crumbs, whole spice fragments, while passing oil at the flow the pump needs. The fine screen is sized well below the nozzle orifice. Both must be confirmed against real strained frying oil from working kitchens before tooling, and the gate is clog-to-failure cycle count, not opinion.

06

Sealing strategy

A removable base doubles the sealing surfaces on a product whose first requirement is "leak-proof". The tension is real but smaller than it looks.

The pre-compression pump pressurises only a small chamber in the head. The body is always at atmospheric pressure. The base seal is therefore static and unpressurised, holding a column of oil 118 mm deep:

p = ρgh = 920 × 9.81 × 0.118 ≈ 1,065 Pa ≈ 0.011 bar

That is the same sealing duty as a moka pot base or a vacuum flask, a solved problem, provided it is solved the right way.

The right way

An axial face gasket, never a thread seal. A captive LSR O-ring sits in a groove in the cap and is compressed flat by a coarse four-start thread. Four starts means the cap cannot cross-thread and closes in a quarter turn. A positive stop and a tactile detent tell the user it is shut, and a moulded lock arrow tells them which way.

Both gaskets, top and base, are user-replaceable and both ship in the ₹199 spares kit. A seal you cannot replace is a product with an expiry date.

IP and compliance

Freedom to operate

The foundational patents are expired. US5455055 and US5650185 ("non-aerosol, uniform spray dispersion system for oil-based products", the Evo lineage) and US5467900 / US5730335 (precompression valve for trigger sprayers) are all early-1990s filings. The base concept of a non-aerosol pre-compression oil sprayer is public domain.

The live risk is AFA Dispensing's Flairosol family. US8905271B2, US9714133, EP2566629B1, with 2010, 2013 priority, running to roughly 2030, 33. Also directly on point and requiring a read: WO2016077114A1, "spray nozzle for high viscosity (e.g. oil) spray applications with uniform spray distribution".

Our strategy avoids the collision entirely: we source the pump head rather than design around the claims. A supplier already selling a pre-compression head has already cleared its own FTO. Our invention sits in the wetted path, which nobody has claimed.

What we file

Provisional patent on the combination that is actually novel: raised anti-sediment intake plus serviceable two-stage filtration plus the dual-opening body. Design registration under the Designs Act 2000 on the final form, ₹1,000 government fee for a startup or small entity with Form-24, plus ₹5,000, 8,000 attorney, giving 10 years extendable by 5. Trademark "Enna" in class 21, plus 8 and 11 as relevant.

The fourth moat nobody files for

India category exclusivity with the pump-head supplier. A contractual moat obtainable in weeks rather than years, and the fastest durable defence available to a first-time hardware brand.

Compliance

Food-contact plastics must conform to the applicable Indian Standard. IS 10146 for polyethylene, with sibling standards per polymer, under the FSS (Packaging & Labelling) Regulations. FSSAI 2.4.1 requires that no substance transfers in quantities that endanger health or alter the food. Migration testing on plastics and elastomers is required.

Open question, do not guess: whether an FSSAI licence applies to an empty food-contact utensil as opposed to a food product is genuinely unclear. Ask a compliance consultant. GSTIN and a GS1 EAN are required regardless for any marketplace listing.

Why the set is one SKU and not two

Blinkit charges ₹25,000 per SKU per state as a listing fee, returned as ad-wallet credit with a 12-month expiry; Zepto and Instamart run comparable structures. Selling the sprayer and decanter in one box with one EAN is what lets a two-piece system survive that. Two SKUs would double the fee in every state.

Even so, at ~₹300 margin each state costs roughly 420 sets of sales just to clear the fee. Sequence accordingly: D2C plus Amazon and Flipkart first, where margin is 41, 49%. Quick commerce in phase two, in two metros, not eight. A ₹1,799 durable is a considered purchase; quick commerce is built for impulse replenishment.

Nobody here

What it tells us

India is the only major market with no domestic brand. China has Supor and Kanbahe, Japan has Mistify, Germany has easySpray, the USA has Evo, Misto and Flairosol. India's shelf is entirely imports and rebadges. That one row is the clearest argument for Enna existing.

The air fryer is the global demand driver. US, Japanese and Chinese listings all lead with it. The premium end goes small (Flairosol 200 ml, Japan 250 ml), which puts our 250 ml sprayer in the right cohort. The filter-in-dip-tube is standard in Japan, validating stage 2 as proven practice. And nobody anywhere sells opaque steel as a kitchen sprayer. Germany codes steel as a grill material. That is whitespace and a teaching problem at once; India is the easiest place on earth to argue it, because steel already is the Indian kitchen material.

Category size, quote the small number

Published estimates range from $572 M to $9.4 B because they measure different things; the big ones count aerosol cooking spray as a food product. The sprayer device category is ≈ $572 M growing 6.4%, heading to ~$1.02 B by 2035. Quoting $9 B and then being asked what it includes is a bad meeting. Bottom-up check: top US listings move 4,000, 30,000 units a month each.

FX: $1 ≈ ₹88 · €1 ≈ ₹96 · ¥1 ≈ ₹0.57. Amazon.de, Amazon.co.jp product pages, JD, Taobao and the German comparison portals were unreachable from the research environment; those entries come from search indexes. Product names and market shape are reliable, verify every price directly before quoting it.

Portion control is the lever in food cost, and oil is specifically called out in Indian cost guidance as an undercounted ingredient. The incumbents are aerosols, PAM Foodservice and Vegalene, and a refillable beats them on arithmetic (≈$48/year against $19 once plus ~$30/year of oil) while cutting overspray drift 50, 70%. In a kitchen, drift is a slip hazard as well as waste.

Four honest problems

1. HACCP labelling is mandatory, decanted containers must carry contents and date, and the format the trade settled on is a silicone band with a dry-erase marker. This is why the grip ring is now writable, and it costs us nothing.

2. A commercial kitchen is an abuse test, 80 °C washers, caustic detergent, daily. Our LSR and POM are specified for domestic duty.

3. Usage is 50, 100× domestic, which breaks the counter. 1,250 strokes is a consumer's four weeks and a banquet kitchen's shift. A pro unit wants 500 ml, 1 L and no counter. It is a different product, not a repackage.

4. Indian 5-star kitchens do not spray much oil. Tadka, deep frying, tandoor and ghee are techniques where oil is a medium, not a coating. The real home is bakery and pastry (pan release, exactly where Vegalene earns its money), grill and à la carte, garde manger, and the calorie-marked menu. Not the Indian section. Say it in the pitch; a chef will respect it and will tell you the same thing anyway.

Recommendation

Phase two, credibility first. Do not build a pro SKU yet. Instead seed 20, 30 units to executive chefs in Chennai and Bangalore, free, for sixty days of real service, in exchange for honest feedback and permission to use the property name. That is ≈ ₹25,000 at landed cost, and it buys abuse data you cannot purchase plus a logo that converts consumer buyers. The route in is the executive chef, not the procurement portal: chefs specify, purchase managers buy.

Design backlog

What is still open, after the modular rebuild. Five items graduated into the specification, two were killed by the fixed nozzle, and what remains is ranked by what it buys against what it costs.

Graduated into the specification

No longer candidates, now committed

Hydrophobic vent membrane in the base cap · spare gasket stored in the base cap recess · repair QR etched on the body · dry erase grip ring · laser etched internal fill line. All five are in section 04 and costed in section 09.

Killed, and correctly

PURGE detent and DRIZZLE mode

Both needed a rotating multi position collar, which puts a moving seal in the oil path and internal channels in a module that has to be serviceable. Pulling the head and flushing it clears a clog in five seconds with no mechanism at all, and the decanter pours. The architecture beat the features.

Tier one

01

A 500 ml body module

The first real payoff of the published interface. One body tool, reusing MOD-01, 02, 03, 05 and 06 completely unchanged, and it answers every reviewer who says 250 ml is too small without splitting the product line or paying a second listing fee on a second head. This is what modularity is for, and it is worth building the interface properly in order to have it.

High value · one tool
02

Nest the set, sprayer docks into the decanter cap

A recess in the decanter cap that the grip ring seats into, so the pair stores as one object. Smaller counter footprint, smaller carton, cheaper shipping, better shelf economics. Now that we are tooling the decanter anyway, this is a feature in a tool we are already cutting rather than a new part. It is also the detail that makes people photograph it.

High value · no new part
03

Tune the counter click to land at stroke completion

ACC-01's ratchet makes a click as a byproduct. Land it precisely at a complete 0.20 ml stroke and it becomes dose confirmation you can hear without watching the pan. Turning an unavoidable noise into proof of the core claim costs a geometry decision, not a part.

High value · no cost
04

Magnetic hob side dock

A steel disc that mounts on a wall or the side of a fridge, and a magnet in the base cap. Answers the question every kitchen object has to answer, which is where it lives, and keeps it off the counter and away from the flame. It is an accessory, so it carries its own price and does not complicate the product.

Medium · accessory

05

Additional nozzle modules

A coarser orifice for vinegar and thin dressings, and a wider cone for large trays. With a bayonet nozzle these are just further part numbers, no retooling of anything else. Modularity already paid for this, which is the argument for the architecture in one line.

Six modules is a cleaning feature and a losing things problem. A small moulded clip that holds the two smallest modules together through a cycle prevents the most likely reason somebody stops fully disassembling it.

A quarter turn that blocks the trigger so it cannot fire in a drawer, a picnic bag or a delivery carton. Removes an entire class of one star review that begins with the words it leaked all over my.
