01

Architecture

Two vessels, one SKU. A 250 ml sprayer and a 750 ml decanter, sized so a one-litre pouch empties into the pair with nothing left over.

The sprayer is open at both ends. The shoulder unscrews on a coarse four-start thread for filling; the base cap unscrews for cleaning. With both off, the body is a straight tube you can see through — which is the only way to genuinely clean an oil vessel, because a bottle brush never reaches the corner it cannot see.

Eleven parts, all tool-free, all wetted parts removable in under fifteen seconds.

03

The anti-clog system

Clogging kills every sprayer on the market, and it has three separate causes, so it gets three separate answers in the order the oil meets them.

Stage 1 — passive. Raised intake, 9 mm standoff

The pickup never touches the floor. Sediment settles into the moat beneath it and is never drawn up. This is fuel-tank logic and it costs nothing: it is a dimension, not a component. It is also the stage that does the most work, because particulate that never enters the tube cannot clog anything downstream.

Stage 2 — serviceable. 600 µm bayonet intake basket

A quarter-turn stainless basket the user pulls out and rinses. The Japanese market already ships filters in the dip tube, which validates the idea — but theirs are sealed, so when they clog they stay clogged. Ours is the same idea made maintainable.

Stage 3 — last defence. 150 µm fine screen

Sits directly behind the orifice, where a single grain ruins the spray cone. Anything that survives stage 2 stops here.

Why 600 and 150 µm

These are design targets, not validated numbers. The intake is sized to stop what survives a normal domestic strainer — besan lumps, crumbs, whole spice fragments — while passing oil at the flow the pump needs. The fine screen is sized well below the nozzle orifice. Both must be confirmed against real strained frying oil from working kitchens before tooling, and the gate is clog-to-failure cycle count, not opinion.

04

Sealing strategy

A removable base doubles the sealing surfaces on a product whose first requirement is "leak-proof". The tension is real but smaller than it looks.

The pre-compression pump pressurises only a small chamber in the head. The body is always at atmospheric pressure. The base seal is therefore static and unpressurised, holding a column of oil 118 mm deep:

p = ρgh = 920 × 9.81 × 0.118 ≈ 1,065 Pa ≈ 0.011 bar

That is the same sealing duty as a moka pot base or a vacuum flask — a solved problem, provided it is solved the right way.

The right way

An axial face gasket, never a thread seal. A captive LSR O-ring sits in a groove in the cap and is compressed flat by a coarse four-start thread. Four starts means the cap cannot cross-thread and closes in a quarter turn. A positive stop and a tactile detent tell the user it is shut, and a moulded lock arrow tells them which way.

Both gaskets — top and base — are user-replaceable and both ship in the ₹199 spares kit. A seal you cannot replace is a product with an expiry date.

Rejected, and why

Glass, Tritan, and clear anything

Glass is the global default and it breaks — the founding brief rules it out. Tritan is safe from a verified supplier but degrades under prolonged UV and offers no light protection. Any clear body accelerates rancidity, which is the failure mode nobody sees until the oil tastes wrong. Valuxe's UV-protected glass is the best answer anyone else has given to this and deserves respect — it still shatters.

Recommendation

Do not tool the decanter

₹80,000 of contingency does not cover a correction cut, and first tools almost always need one. A 750 ml opaque stainless bottle with a wide mouth is a commodity — vendors in Palghar and Gujarat make them by the container load. Source it, brand it, and return the whole ₹2.2 lakh to contingency, taking it to ₹3 lakh. The sprayer is the invention; the decanter is a bottle. It also shortens the timeline, because only one product needs new tools.

Supplier shortlist — directory leads, nobody called

Injection moulding, Bangalore (Peenya belt): Kruger Industries — ISO 9001:2015, operating since 2004, nine machines, in-house mould-flow analysis, which matters for the pump housing. Fortune Group — quotes 5–90 g engineering plastics, exactly our part band. Mathru Toolings — tool development plus moulding. Shree Sampath Plastics — second-source candidate.

Stainless bodies — probably not Bangalore. India's stainless bottle industry sits in Maharashtra (Palghar/Wada), Gujarat and Delhi NCR, not Karnataka. Names surfaced: Eagle Consumer Products, NIRLON Kitchenware. Plan on a split supply chain and accept it rather than forcing geography — deep drawing a body with threads at both ends is a specialist operation, and the vendors who do it at volume already do it for flasks.

Qualification checklist before anyone gets a rupee

Food-contact material declaration and prior BIS/IS work · samples at our part size, not a deck · scrap rate on a comparable part · a written T1 first-shot date · tool ownership stated in the PO, because the tool is ours and must be movable · second-source the nozzle and seals from day one.

09

IP and compliance

Freedom to operate

The foundational patents are expired. US5455055 and US5650185 ("non-aerosol, uniform spray dispersion system for oil-based products", the Evo lineage) and US5467900 / US5730335 (precompression valve for trigger sprayers) are all early-1990s filings. The base concept of a non-aerosol pre-compression oil sprayer is public domain.

The live risk is AFA Dispensing's Flairosol family — US8905271B2, US9714133, EP2566629B1 — with 2010–2013 priority, running to roughly 2030–33. Also directly on point and requiring a read: WO2016077114A1, "spray nozzle for high viscosity (e.g. oil) spray applications with uniform spray distribution".

Our strategy avoids the collision entirely: we source the pump head rather than design around the claims. A supplier already selling a pre-compression head has already cleared its own FTO. Our invention sits in the wetted path, which nobody has claimed.

What we file

Provisional patent on the combination that is actually novel: raised anti-sediment intake plus serviceable two-stage filtration plus the dual-opening body. Design registration under the Designs Act 2000 on the final form — ₹1,000 government fee for a startup or small entity with Form-24, plus ₹5,000–8,000 attorney, giving 10 years extendable by 5. Trademark "Enna" in class 21, plus 8 and 11 as relevant.

The fourth moat nobody files for

India category exclusivity with the pump-head supplier. A contractual moat obtainable in weeks rather than years, and the fastest durable defence available to a first-time hardware brand.

Compliance

Food-contact plastics must conform to the applicable Indian Standard — IS 10146 for polyethylene, with sibling standards per polymer — under the FSS (Packaging & Labelling) Regulations. FSSAI 2.4.1 requires that no substance transfers in quantities that endanger health or alter the food. Migration testing on plastics and elastomers is required.

Open question, do not guess: whether an FSSAI licence applies to an empty food-contact utensil as opposed to a food product is genuinely unclear. Ask a compliance consultant. GSTIN and a GS1 EAN are required regardless for any marketplace listing.

Why the set is one SKU and not two

Blinkit charges ₹25,000 per SKU per state as a listing fee, returned as ad-wallet credit with a 12-month expiry; Zepto and Instamart run comparable structures. Selling the sprayer and decanter in one box with one EAN is what lets a two-piece system survive that. Two SKUs would double the fee in every state.

Even so, at ~₹300 margin each state costs roughly 420 sets of sales just to clear the fee. Sequence accordingly: D2C plus Amazon and Flipkart first, where margin is 41–49%. Quick commerce in phase two, in two metros, not eight. A ₹1,799 durable is a considered purchase; quick commerce is built for impulse replenishment.

What it tells us

India is the only major market with no domestic brand. China has Supor and Kanbahe, Japan has Mistify, Germany has easySpray, the USA has Evo, Misto and Flairosol. India's shelf is entirely imports and rebadges. That one row is the clearest argument for Enna existing.

The air fryer is the global demand driver — US, Japanese and Chinese listings all lead with it. The premium end goes small (Flairosol 200 ml, Japan 250 ml), which puts our 250 ml sprayer in the right cohort. The filter-in-dip-tube is standard in Japan, validating stage 2 as proven practice. And nobody anywhere sells opaque steel as a kitchen sprayer — Germany codes steel as a grill material. That is whitespace and a teaching problem at once; India is the easiest place on earth to argue it, because steel already is the Indian kitchen material.

Category size — quote the small number

Published estimates range from $572 M to $9.4 B because they measure different things; the big ones count aerosol cooking spray as a food product. The sprayer device category is ≈ $572 M growing 6.4%, heading to ~$1.02 B by 2035. Quoting $9 B and then being asked what it includes is a bad meeting. Bottom-up check: top US listings move 4,000–30,000 units a month each.

FX: $1 ≈ ₹88 · €1 ≈ ₹96 · ¥1 ≈ ₹0.57. Amazon.de, Amazon.co.jp product pages, JD, Taobao and the German comparison portals were unreachable from the research environment; those entries come from search indexes. Product names and market shape are reliable — verify every price directly before quoting it.

Portion control is the lever in food cost, and oil is specifically called out in Indian cost guidance as an undercounted ingredient. The incumbents are aerosols — PAM Foodservice and Vegalene — and a refillable beats them on arithmetic (≈$48/year against $19 once plus ~$30/year of oil) while cutting overspray drift 50–70%. In a kitchen, drift is a slip hazard as well as waste.

Four honest problems

1. HACCP labelling is mandatory — decanted containers must carry contents and date, and the format the trade settled on is a silicone band with a dry-erase marker. This is why the grip ring is now writable, and it costs us nothing.

2. A commercial kitchen is an abuse test — 80 °C washers, caustic detergent, daily. Our LSR and POM are specified for domestic duty.

3. Usage is 50–100× domestic, which breaks the counter. 1,250 strokes is a consumer's four weeks and a banquet kitchen's shift. A pro unit wants 500 ml–1 L and no counter. It is a different product, not a repackage.

4. Indian 5-star kitchens do not spray much oil. Tadka, deep frying, tandoor and ghee are techniques where oil is a medium, not a coating. The real home is bakery and pastry (pan release — exactly where Vegalene earns its money), grill and à la carte, garde manger, and the calorie-marked menu. Not the Indian section. Say it in the pitch; a chef will respect it and will tell you the same thing anyway.

Recommendation

Phase two, credibility first. Do not build a pro SKU yet. Instead seed 20–30 units to executive chefs in Chennai and Bangalore — free, for sixty days of real service, in exchange for honest feedback and permission to use the property name. That is ≈ ₹25,000 at landed cost, and it buys abuse data you cannot purchase plus a logo that converts consumer buyers. The route in is the executive chef, not the procurement portal: chefs specify, purchase managers buy.

Design backlog

Candidate features beyond what is already specified. Ranked by what they buy against what they cost. The first six are the ones I would fight for.

Tier one — worth tooling for

01

PURGE position on the nozzle collar

A fourth detent that opens a bypass so you can pump hot water — or a little fresh oil — straight through the nozzle to clear it, without taking anything apart. Clogging is the number one cause of death in this category, and every rescue today means disassembly, a toothpick and fifteen minutes. We are already tooling a multi-position collar, so this is one more detent and an internal channel. It is also the single most demonstrable feature in a thirty-second video.

High value · low cost
02

Hydrophobic vent membrane in the base cap

As oil leaves, air must enter. Every sprayer on the market pulls raw kitchen air — humid, greasy, airborne particulate — straight into the headspace, which is exactly what oxidises oil. A PTFE vent membrane costs a few paise, is standard in food packaging, and nobody in this category uses one. It is invisible, it directly serves the rancidity argument that justifies the opaque body, and it is the most patentable idea on this list in combination with the rest.

High value · low cost
03

Make DRIZZLE a real controlled pour

The global bestsellers are all "2-in-1 spray and pour" — that is the format buyers expect, because sometimes you genuinely want a tablespoon. If the DRIZZLE detent delivers a clean controlled stream rather than a coarse spray, we match the category convention without a second bottle and without compromising the mist. Not having this is a reason to not buy us.

High value · low cost
04

Nest the set — sprayer docks into the decanter cap

A recess in the decanter's cap that the sprayer's base ring seats into, so the two store as one object. Smaller counter footprint, smaller carton, cheaper shipping, better shelf economics in quick commerce — and it makes the pair read as a designed system rather than a bundle. This is the detail that makes people photograph it.

High value · medium cost
05

Make the counter click audible and deliberate

The counter ratchet produces a click as a byproduct. Tune it so the click lands precisely at the completion of a full 0.20 ml stroke, and it becomes dose confirmation you can hear without looking at the pan. Turning an unavoidable noise into the proof of the core claim costs a geometry decision, not a part.

High value · no cost
06

Store a spare gasket inside the base cap

A moulded recess in the base cap holds one spare LSR face gasket. When the seal eventually needs replacing, the replacement is already inside the product. It costs a feature in a tool we are cutting anyway, and it makes "buy once, own for life" a thing you can physically show someone rather than a claim on a website.

Medium value · no cost

Tier two — cheap, worth considering

07

Second nozzle in the box, for vinegar and water

A larger orifice for low-viscosity liquids turns one product into a dressing and misting tool. One extra moulded part, and it answers the "can I use it for vinegar" question every listing in this category gets.

Medium · low cost
08

Colour-coded grip rings, sold as a three-pack

Yellow, green and red LSR rings so a household runs gingelly, groundnut and vinegar without confusion — and so a commercial kitchen can colour-code, which is standard HACCP practice. Near-zero marginal cost on a tool we already have, and it creates a repeat-purchase accessory alongside the spares kit.

Medium · no new tool
09

Trigger lock

A quarter-turn that blocks the trigger so it cannot fire in a drawer, a picnic bag or a delivery carton. Small, and it removes an entire class of one-star review that begins "it leaked all over my…".

Medium · low cost
10

Dishwasher clip for the small parts

Eleven tool-free parts is a cleaning feature and a losing-things problem. A small moulded clip that holds the screens, valve and basket together through a dishwasher cycle costs almost nothing and prevents the most likely reason someone stops fully disassembling it.

Medium · low cost
11

Print the cold limit on the collar

"Below 24 °C, coconut oil will not spray" belongs on the product, at the moment of the decision — not in a manual nobody keeps. It costs a line of marking and it converts our most awkward limitation into visible honesty.

Low cost
12

Thumb detent on the trigger

A shallow recess so the hand lands identically every time. Repeatable grip means repeatable stroke force, which is what makes a metered dose actually metered. A surface detail that quietly supports the headline claim.

Low cost
13

QR on the base for spares and refills

Laser-etched alongside the fill line, linking to the spares kit and the dose reference. The spares business only works if reordering takes ten seconds, and this is the cheapest customer-retention mechanism available.

Low cost
If you only do three

Purge, vent membrane, and a real pour mode

Purge attacks the category's number one failure mode and demos in seconds. The vent membrane is invisible, nearly free, uniquely ours, and strengthens the patent. A real pour mode removes the one reason a buyer comparing us against a 2-in-1 glass bottle would pick the glass bottle.

Roadmap

01

Category teardown and failure analysis

Complete. Mechanism architectures compared, failure modes review-mined across Evo, Misto and the import field, five markets surveyed, India-specific constraints documented.

02

Freedom to operate and provisional filing · 4 weeks

Read the Flairosol claim set and WO2016077114A1 properly. File a provisional on the filtration, raised intake, dual-opening and vent membrane combination. File the Enna word mark in class 21.

03

Pump head sourcing and India exclusivity · months 2–4

Qualify pre-compression heads against the 25–95 cSt window, then negotiate exclusive Indian rights — the fastest durable moat available.

04

Prototypes and the validation gates · months 3–6

Printed bodies and screens against real strained frying oil from working kitchens. Clog-to-failure count and the inverted leak test are the pass/fail gates. Seed 20–30 units to executive chefs in parallel.

05

Tooling, compliance and first run · months 6–9

Design registration on the final form, migration testing under the BIS standards, GSTIN and GS1 barcodes, soft tooling for a first thousand sets.
