Open the top, not the neck.

Architecture · bottom

And open the bottom.

Twist the silicone grip and the base comes off. Now the body is an open tube you can look straight through — drop it in hot water, run a brush clean through it, put it in the dishwasher. No bottle brush ever reaches the corner it can't see.

The hard problem

Eleven parts. Every one washable.

Sprayers don't die of old age — they die of clogging. Every wetted part here comes out by hand in under fifteen seconds. No tools, no captive screens, no sealed cartridge you throw away.

The gauge

It counts, so you don't have to.

No window, no glass, no hole in the wall to leak from. A mechanical counter in the head ticks once per stroke — the same movement an inhaler uses. Because every stroke is exactly 0.20 ml, the bar is at once a fuel gauge and a calorie meter. A fill line etched inside the mouth handles the other half — knowing when to stop pouring.

1,250 strokes · etched 250 ml fill line

The set

One pouch. Nothing left over.

A 500 ml sprayer full of oil weighs 855 g, and squeezing a trigger on that is tiring by the third pan. So we split it. The sprayer holds 250 ml and stays light in the hand; the decanter holds the other 750 ml in opaque steel. Together they are exactly one litre — exactly one pouch.

Where the name comes from

"என்ன வேணும்?"

In Tamil, enna means what. Move one sound and எண்ணெய் — ennai — means oil. There is a comedy scene every Tamil household can recite, built entirely on those two words refusing to meet. We named the product after the collision.

Customer எண்ணெய் வேணும்! eṇṇai vēṇum! Oil is what I want!
Shopkeeper அதத்தானே கேக்குறேன் — என்ன வேணும்னு? adhaththāne kēkkuṟēn — enna vēṇum-nu? That is exactly what I am asking — what do you want?

Why a sprayer at all

The same pan. Four ways to oil it.

This is the entire argument for the product, and it is just arithmetic. Cooking oil carries about 9 kcal per gram and weighs about 0.92 g per millilitre — so calories are simply a question of how many millilitres end up in the pan.

One Enna stroke meters 0.20 ml — 0.18 g — about 1.7 kcal. Coating a 26 cm pan takes eight to ten strokes. The claim isn't that a spray is magic; it's that a spray is measurable, and a pour never is.

The India problem

Three stages against the clog.

Indian kitchens strain and reuse frying oil, and what comes back carries besan, crumbs and masala. That particulate is what kills every sprayer on the market inside a month. It has three separate causes, so Enna answers it in three separate places — in the order the oil meets them.

What we fixed

Ten complaints, ten answers.

Each of these is a documented failure in reviews of the sprayers already on sale — the ₹300 imports and the ₹2,600 imports alike. We treated the review sections as a specification.

01

It clogs in two weeks

Three-stage filtration with a raised intake, so the particulate never reaches the pump in the first place.

02

It sprays a jet, not a mist

A pre-compression valve holds a 2.4 bar floor under every discharge. Squeeze slowly and it still atomises — or it doesn't fire at all.

03

It leaks at the thread

Coarse four-start threads that cannot cross-thread, closing onto silicone face seals rather than thread-on-thread contact.

04

You can't clean inside it

Both ends open. The body becomes a straight tube you can see through, brush through and drop in hot water.

05

Oil runs down the outside

The nozzle is recessed in a wiper collar with a return channel that drains the post-spray bead back into the body.

06

The plastic top warped

Steel and silicone in every part that faces the hob. Nothing near the heat is structural plastic.

07

It's a mess to refill

The pouch goes into the decanter once. The decanter tops up the sprayer three times. Nothing is ever poured from a floppy pouch again.

08

You never know what's left

A segmented stroke counter in the head, not a window. No hole in the wall means no leak path — and it counts calories while it's there.

09

You overfilled it and killed the pump

Oil above the max line overloads the pump on almost every sprayer sold. A 250 ml line laser-etched on the inside wall is visible straight down the open mouth while you pour.

10

Nobody answers the warranty

Spares sold openly at ₹199 — nozzle, both gaskets, both screens — so "buy once" means something past year two.

Honestly

What Enna will not do.

Every sprayer sold in India today claims to handle every oil. None of them do. We would rather draw the line ourselves than let you discover it in January.

Solid coconut oil

Coconut oil sets below about 24 °C. No sprayer on earth atomises a solid. Warm the bottle in a bowl of hot water, or use it in the months it stays liquid.

Ghee

Solid at Indian room temperature for much of the year. Enna is built for liquid oils — mustard, groundnut, gingelly, sunflower, rice bran. Ghee belongs on a spoon.

Unstrained frying oil

The filtration is designed for what survives a normal kitchen strainer, not for oil poured straight back out of the kadai with the crumbs still in it.

Make old oil fresh

An opaque steel body slows ageing — it doesn't stop it. The sprayer holds about four weeks of cooking and the decanter keeps the rest in the dark, which is the best either of us can do. Buy the pouch you will actually finish.

Who this is for

People who already measure.

Three groups reach for Enna first, and they have exactly one thing in common: every one of them already counts something. Enna does not ask them to start a new habit — it plugs into the one they have.

Measures blood sugar

Diabetic and PCOS households

Enormous in India and medically motivated rather than aspirational. Fat quantity is already on the doctor's list, and a counter that reports the week's oil in kilocalories is the first honest answer anyone has given them.

Measures cooking

Air fryer owners

They already bought an appliance to use less oil, then went back to pouring from the bottle into the basket. The argument is pre-made; Enna is the missing accessory.

Measures intake

Calorie and macro trackers

Cooking oil is the single largest untracked number in an Indian food log — nobody knows what went into the sambar. 1,250 strokes at 1.7 kcal each is the first oil figure they can actually enter.

Launch geography and launch audience are separate questions. These three are pan-India; the name, the joke and the oils argue for Tamil Nadu as the first state on the shelf.
