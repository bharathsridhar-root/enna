# Enna — The 5-Star Hotel and Food Service Opportunity (Round 4)

## 1. How big the room is

| | Figure |
|---|---|
| Classified **5-star hotels in India** (Apr 2025) | **823** |
| IHCL / Taj portfolio (early 2026) | 615 hotels — 360 operational, 255 pipeline |
| ITC Hotels | 140+ hotels across 90 destinations |
| Marriott share of Indian branded rooms | ≈ 13.6% (largest); IHCL ≈ 12% |

| India cloud kitchen market | $1.13 B (2024) → $2.84 B (2030), 16.6% CAGR |
| Rebel Foods alone | **4,000+ kitchens across 70 cities** |
| India QSR growth | ~15% CAGR |
| Indian restaurant food cost benchmark | **28–35% of revenue** (fine dining higher) |

A 5-star property does not have one kitchen. It has six to twelve — banquet,
each specialty restaurant, bakery and pastry, garde manger, room service, staff
canteen. At even 20–30 units per property, **823 properties is a 20,000-unit
addressable base**, before cloud kitchens and QSR.

## 2. Why the pain is real

Portion control is *the* lever in food cost, and the standard illustration is
brutal: **a 10 g over-portion across 200 covers gives away 2 kg of product free,
every single day.** Oil is specifically called out in Indian cost-control
guidance as an **undercounted** ingredient — it is left out of recipe costings
along with garnishes and condiments.

Meanwhile the professional alternative today is aerosol. **PAM Foodservice**
(ConAgra) and **Vegalene** (PLZ Corp, "the leading branded pan spray in
foodservice", a patented 3-oil blend claiming up to 30% more release agents)
own that shelf, alongside Bak-Klene and Everbake. Against them:

- Aerosol runs roughly **$48/year** versus a refillable at **$19 once plus
  ~$30/year of oil** — comparable in year one, cheaper every year after.
- A pump sprayer's narrower cone **cuts overspray drift by 50–70%** against an
  aerosol in side-by-side testing. In a kitchen, drift is not just waste — it is
  a slip hazard and a cleaning cost.
- Aerosol cans are consumables with a supply chain; a refillable is capex.

Food-grade pressurised pump sprayers already exist for commercial grills and
kitchens, so the concept has a foothold. Nobody has brought the engineering.

## 3. Four honest problems

**1. HACCP labelling is mandatory, and our product has nowhere to write.**
Any decanted container in a commercial kitchen must be identifiable at all
times — contents, date, and where relevant allergen status, in a form that
survives service and satisfies an inspector. The format the trade has settled on
is **a silicone band with a dry- or wet-erase marker.**

> **Design consequence, and it is nearly free:** make the silicone grip ring a
> **writable dry-erase surface**. We already have that part, in that material,
> in that position. It turns a consumer component into the HACCP compliance
> feature — and it is a genuine consumer feature too, because dating your oil is
> exactly what you should do to manage rancidity.

**2. A commercial kitchen is an abuse test.** Dropped on tile, thrown in the
dish pit, through an 80 °C commercial washer with caustic detergent, every day.
Our LSR gaskets and POM internals are specified for domestic duty. A pro SKU
needs its own validation and possibly different materials.

**3. Usage is 50–100× domestic, which breaks the counter.** 1,250 strokes is a
consumer's four weeks. A banquet kitchen could burn that in a shift. A
professional unit wants 500 ml–1 L and probably no counter at all. **The pro
product is a different product, not a repackage** — and pretending otherwise is
how hardware startups lose a year.

**4. The honest one: Indian 5-star kitchens do not spray much oil.** At that
level, Indian cuisine runs on tadka, deep frying, tandoor and ghee — techniques
where oil is a *medium*, not a coating. The sprayer's real home inside a hotel
is narrower than the room-count suggests:

- **Bakery and pastry** — pan release, the single strongest fit, and exactly
  where Vegalene and Bak-Klene earn their money
- **Grill and à la carte** — brushing oil onto proteins and vegetables
- **Garde manger / salads** — dressing without drowning
- **The "healthy" or calorie-marked menu** — increasingly standard in Indian
  5-stars and the one place the calorie story sells itself
- **Continental and Asian sections**, not the Indian section

Say that out loud in the pitch. A chef will respect it and will tell you the
same thing in the first meeting anyway.

## 4. How the channel actually works

India's HORECA procurement runs through dedicated B2B platforms — **Hospiverse,
India Hospitality Hub, HorecaStop, KitchenaryKart, FSIPL** — plus direct
distributor relationships.

The decision pattern that matters: **chefs specify, purchase managers buy.**
Owners are explicitly advised to ask their chefs what to buy. Purchase managers
compare multiple suppliers, **demand samples before bulk orders**, and enforce
delivery SLAs. Mumbai's purchase managers are described as among the most
sophisticated in the country.

So the route in is the executive chef, not the procurement portal.

## 5. Recommendation

**HORECA is a phase-two, credibility-first play. Do not build a pro SKU yet.**

The sales cycle is slow, the abuse requirements are different, the product needs
to change, and a startup that needs early cash should not be chasing purchase
managers. But do **one** thing now, because it is cheap and the returns are
asymmetric:

> **Seed 20–30 units to executive chefs** at named properties in Chennai and
> Bangalore, free, in exchange for two things: honest feedback after 60 days of
> real service, and permission to use the property name.

That costs roughly **₹25,000 at landed cost**. In return you get:

1. **Abuse data you cannot buy.** Sixty days in a hotel kitchen is worth two
   years of domestic testing for finding what breaks.
2. **Logos.** A Taj or ITC name on the site is worth more than any ad budget a
   first-time brand can afford, and it converts *consumer* buyers, not just
   trade ones.
3. **Product truth.** Chefs will tell you within a week whether the spray
   pattern, the trigger force and the cleaning cycle actually work. They will
   not be polite about it.

Then make the **one design change now**: the writable silicone ring. It costs
nothing, it is required for any future HORECA sale, and it is a real consumer
feature today.
