# Enna — Product Brief (as stated by the founder, Round 1)

## The name

**Enna** — from எண்ணெய் (*ennai*), Tamil for oil. Short, rhymes, works as a
consumer brand in and outside Tamil Nadu.

## The thesis

Portion control by atomisation. A spray of oil is ~15 kcal. A teaspoon or
tablespoon poured is 40–150 kcal. Free-pouring from a bottle can put 200–300 kcal
into a single pan. The sprayer is not a gadget — it is a behaviour-change device
that makes the healthy portion the default one. The mission is as much awareness
as it is hardware.

## The gap in the market

Two bad options exist today:
- **Cheap imports** (Temu, AliExpress, the Amazon.in / Flipkart mass market):
  plasticky, clog within weeks, leak, break.
- **Premium Western** (Evo, ~$30 / ₹2,600): patented, well-known, but does not
  justify the price — still clogs, still hard to clean, glass breaks, and it was
  never designed for Indian oils or Indian kitchens.

Nobody is building a genuinely well-engineered sprayer for the Indian kitchen.

## Requested requirements

**Must-have**
1. Easy to clean — no greasy residue that can't be reached; oil film is the
   enemy. Repeated refilling is assumed.
2. Easy to open and fill — current products open through a neck too narrow to
   pour into or clean through.
3. Leak-proof and shatter-proof; must not break.
4. Good material — not glass (breaks), not cheap plastic.
5. Silicone grip on the base so it does not wobble or get knocked over.
6. A spray mechanism that works across **different oil densities**.
7. **Anti-clog design for particulate matter** — Indians reuse oil, and carried-
   over solids block the dip tube and nozzle. This is the hardest and most
   important requirement.
8. Integrated funnel-style refilling for topping up when empty.

**Good-to-have**
9. A level indicator.

**Price/performance**: highest possible ratio. Buy once, own for life.

## Deliverables the founder wants

1. Deep market research across Amazon (US/IN/JP), Flipkart, Bed Bath & Beyond,
   and global retail — extract the best features that already exist.
2. A beautiful showcase website with Apple-style 3D CAD parts that explode and
   extrude on scroll.
3. A bill of materials and a manufacturing process.
4. A route to actually manufacture and deliver.
5. Patent and trademark strategy.

# Design directions (proposed — for discussion)

## A. Architecture: "open the top, not the neck"

The whole shoulder unscrews on a wide (Ø45–50 mm) coarse thread, thermos-style,
so the body becomes an open cup. This one move solves three stated problems at
once: filling needs no funnel (the mouth *is* the funnel), a hand and brush reach
every internal surface, and a coarse multi-start thread with a silicone face seal
cannot cross-thread the way Evo's fine thread does. The pump becomes a cartridge
that drops into the open mouth.

## B. The anti-clog system (the core invention, and the patentable part)

Three independent stages, because clogging has three independent causes:

1. **Elevated / floating intake.** The dip tube does not touch the base. Sediment
   settles into a moat below the pickup, exactly as a fuel-tank pickup does.
   Optionally a float-weighted intake that draws from just below the surface,
   which is the cleanest oil in the bottle.
2. **Coarse debris screen** at the intake — a removable, bayonet-fit stainless
   mesh basket sized to stop besan, crumbs and masala. User-serviceable and
   rinsable, unlike the sealed filters in Japanese units.
3. **Fine screen immediately before the nozzle**, protecting the orifice from
   anything that gets past stage 2.

## C. Consistent mist across viscosities

A **pre-compression valve** with a preset cracking pressure, so the nozzle only
ever fires above the atomisation threshold — this is precisely what separates the
Flairosol's mist from a cheap sprayer's squirt, and it is the fix for the
"sprays a jet after the first use" complaint. Pair it with an adjustable collar:
**MIST / SPRAY / DRIZZLE**, so thick oils and ghee get a coarser setting rather
than failing. Must be designed around, or licensed from, AFA's live patent family.

## D. Anti-drip and the greasy-neck problem

Recessed nozzle inside a wiper collar, with a return channel that drains the
post-spray bead back into the body instead of down the outside. The silicone base
ring doubles as a drip catcher and can be peeled off and washed.

## E. Materials

Opaque 18/8 stainless body (blocks light, roughly doubles oil shelf life, will
not shatter, will not distort next to the hob). Food-grade silicone seals and
base. POM/PP pump internals. If a level indicator is wanted, a narrow vertical
window strip rather than a clear bottle — keep the light out.

## F. Capacity

150–200 ml, deliberately smaller than the 470–500 ml units flooding the market.
Oil should be finished in three or four weeks; a big bottle is a rancidity
feature disguised as a value feature. This is a claim we should be willing to
defend publicly.

## G. Calibrated dose

Each stroke delivers a known mass of oil, so it maps to a known calorie count.
This is simultaneously the marketing story and a real feature — it turns the
mission statement into something printed on the product.

## H. Lifetime ownership

"Buy once" requires spares. A ₹99–₹149 service kit — nozzle, seals, screens —
sold openly, plus full tool-free disassembly of every wetted part.
